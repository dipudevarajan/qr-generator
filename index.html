Common questions

Answers to the things people actually ask.

Is adfreeqr.com really free?

Yes. No sign-up, no credit card, no upgrade prompts, no watermarks. The source code is on GitHub under the MIT license — you can run your own copy on your own server if you'd rather. We don't even ask for your email.

Do my QR codes expire?

No. The QR code you download encodes your URL directly — it works forever, even if our site disappears tomorrow. Compare that to "dynamic QR codes" from paid services: those route through their server, so when they shut down or your subscription lapses, every printed code becomes useless.

Can I add my logo to the QR code?

Yes. Upload any PNG, SVG, or JPG — we place it in the centre of the QR with a white halo for visibility. Error correction is set to level H, which lets the QR survive up to 30% obstruction, so the code still scans reliably with a logo covering the middle.

What is a multi-link page and how does it work?

A small landing page with several links — your name, a logo, and a list of URLs (Instagram, website, WhatsApp, etc.). Build it in the generator, then either let us host it free at adfreeqr.com/p/... (the link data is encoded in the URL itself — no database, never expires) or download the HTML and host it yourself.

Do you track me?

We count anonymous events: which type of QR was generated and the visiting country (from a Cloudflare HTTP header). We never store raw IP addresses, never set cookies, never use a third-party analytics provider. Generation itself happens in your browser — your URLs and logos are never sent to our servers.

Is the QR generated on my device or your server?

Entirely on your device. The whole page is one HTML file and runs in your browser using JavaScript. Open it once, then disconnect from the internet — the generator still works. You can verify this in the browser dev tools network tab.

Can I use these QR codes commercially?

Yes. QR codes you generate are yours — print them on products, packaging, ads, books, t-shirts. There's no licence, no attribution requirement, no restriction.
